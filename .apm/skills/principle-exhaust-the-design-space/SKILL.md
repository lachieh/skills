# Exhaust the Design Space

When a consequential design has multiple viable shapes, build two or three
meaningfully distinct prototypes or sketches and compare them side by side
before committing. A variation of the first idea does not count as another
design.

## Apply it

Use this principle for novel user interactions, architecture without established
precedent, product decisions that depend on feel, and questions concrete
observation can resolve better than discussion.

A prototype should be cheap to discard and complete enough to answer the design
question. It may be a UI, code path, interface sketch, data model, benchmark, or
another executable comparison.

## Compare alternatives

Compare each design against the user's goal, the outcome we want for them,
constraints, failure modes, implementation and maintenance cost, and the
resulting experience.

Mechanical work with an established pattern does not need design exploration.
Procedures such as `architect`, `arena`, and prototype workflows operationalize
this principle.
