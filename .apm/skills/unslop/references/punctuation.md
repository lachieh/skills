# Punctuation

Punctuation exposes sentence structure. It does not manufacture rhythm or
emphasis.

- Do not use em dashes.
- Do not replace them with spaced hyphens or en dashes.
- Use straight quotes in repository prose.
- Use colons to introduce lists, examples, definitions, output, and label-value
  pairs.
- Avoid colons as dramatic mid-sentence connectors.
- Use parentheses for versions, units, citations, and short information that is
  genuinely secondary.
- Prefer two sentences over a long parenthetical aside.
- Avoid semicolons when two sentences are clearer.
- Use semicolons in complex lists when commas would be ambiguous.
- Use hyphens for compound words and ASCII ranges, not as sentence punctuation.
- Use exclamation marks sparingly for genuine thanks, readiness, or product
  voice.
- Preserve punctuation inside code, commands, paths, quotations, and generated
  output.
